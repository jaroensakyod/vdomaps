# "The Lawn Chair That Went to 16,000 Feet" — original script v1 (2026-11-09)
Format: cartoon-3d-short skill (2D characters in a 3D world), ~60 s, ~3.7 words/s, English, one steady voice.

## Facts (sources: Wikipedia "Lawnchair Larry flight"; Guinness World Records news 2025-05; HistoryNet)
- 2 July 1982, backyard in San Pedro, California. Larry Walters, truck driver, no pilot training;
  poor eyesight kept him from becoming a pilot. Plan: float over the Mojave Desert, pop balloons to descend.
- Lawn chair + 42 helium weather balloons (sometimes quoted 43). Plastic jugs of water as ballast.
- Took a pellet gun (to pop balloons and come down), CB radio, sandwiches, soda, camera.
- The cord tying the chair to his Jeep broke prematurely; he rose fast to about 16,000 ft (estimate),
  far higher than intended, into controlled airspace near Long Beach Airport; spotted from two commercial airliners.
- Shot several balloons to descend, then dropped the pellet gun overboard.
- Dangling cables caught a power line in Long Beach (423 E 44th Way); the line broke, 20-minute blackout. He was unhurt.
- FAA proposed $4,000 in penalties; the airworthiness charge was dropped (no certificate class fits a lawn chair);
  he paid $1,500.
- The chair is now in the National Air and Space Museum.

## Script v2 (facts corrected 2026-11-09, approved)
[HOOK] In nineteen eighty-two, a truck driver flew sixteen thousand feet into the sky. In a lawn chair.
[SETTING] Larry had wanted to fly his whole life, but his eyesight was too poor to become a pilot.
So in his backyard in California, he tied forty-two weather balloons to a garden chair.
[ACTION] He packed sandwiches, a soda, a radio, and a pellet gun.
The plan was simple: drift gently over the desert, then pop a few balloons to come back down.
Then the cord tying his chair to his Jeep snapped.
He didn't drift up gently. He shot up like a cork.
One thousand feet. Five thousand. Sixteen thousand, freezing cold, right in the airspace of a busy airport,
where two airliners reported a man in a lawn chair.
[SNAP] Larry shot a few balloons, started sinking, and then dropped the gun.
[DOUBLE] Luckily, he'd already popped enough to come down.
Unluckily, he came down right onto a power line.
[EXPLAIN] Here's why he went up so fast. Ever tried to hold a beach ball under water? The water shoves it back up.
Air does the same thing to helium. Forty-two beach balls, all pushing up at once.
[CONSEQUENCE] The aviation authority fined him fifteen hundred dollars, but had to drop one charge,
because there is no licence for flying a lawn chair.
[CALLBACK] Today that chair sits in the National Air and Space Museum, next to real aircraft.
